## What is this study about?
You will play a short map-based decision game together with three other participants. The game consists of three rounds.

## What data is collected?
- Your actions in the game (which areas you propose or decide on) and when you take them.
- How you use the interface (e.g. which areas you look at, how long info panels are open, whether the game window is in focus).
<!-- if audio -->
- **A recording of your microphone**: your voice and what is said in the room (see *Audio recording*).
<!-- end if -->

**No names, e-mail addresses, IP addresses or device identifiers are stored** by the game. Your data are stored under a random code that is shown to you at the end of the game.
<!-- if audio -->
Of your microphone, only its technical settings are stored (its sample rate and whether the browser filtered the sound), never its name or id. If you or others say a name aloud, it is in the recording.
<!-- end if -->

Data are stored encrypted on the researcher's computer and are never uploaded to the internet during the session. Results are reported only in aggregate or under the random code.

## Background questions
After this form you answer a few background questions (age bracket, field of study, experience). Every question offers "prefer not to say". Your answers are stored under the same random code as your game data.

## Audio recording
- **When:** your browser records your microphone from your microphone check until the game is complete: during the calibration, all rounds, breaks and game pauses. The microphone check comes after the briefing. You cannot mute yourself. The researcher can pause the recording for everyone, for example when someone else enters the room.
- **No microphone opens before everyone is ready.** The microphone checks start only once every participant in the room has agreed to this form and finished the briefing.
- **Other voices:** all participants sit in the same room, so your microphone also picks up the others, and theirs pick up you.
- **Calibration:** before the first round, each participant reads a short sentence aloud in turn while the others stay quiet. This measures how loud each voice is on each microphone. It starts once everyone has said they are ready for it.
- **Storage:** the recordings are stored encrypted on the researcher's computer, each file with its own key, under your random code. They are never uploaded to the internet during the session. [Placeholder: who may listen to the recordings.]
- **Who spoke when:** this is worked out only by comparing how loud the sound is on each microphone. Your voice is never used to recognise you (no voiceprints).

## Taking part and withdrawal
Taking part is voluntary. You may stop at any time, without giving a reason. After the session you can ask for your data to be removed by quoting your code to the contact address shown above. Once data have been fully anonymised (see the retention statement above) they can no longer be linked to you and can therefore no longer be removed.

## Future research
With your permission, your pseudonymised data may be reused in future research by the same research group. This is optional: you can take part without it.
